Your Job Search Effectiveness Report

Sample participant report based on a hypothetical 15-minute interview.

Executive Summary

You have a well-defined target, but your search behavior has become broader and more automated as the search has continued.

The strongest signal from your interview is that your successful applications look meaningfully different from your typical applications. The opportunities that produced interviews tended to be stronger-fit roles, involved a relationship or direct connection, and received more deliberate positioning. Your higher-volume cold applications received much less attention and have produced relatively little response.

The biggest opportunity is not to apply more. It is to shift more of your existing effort toward the behaviors already associated with your interviews.

What We Heard About Your Search

You are primarily targeting Director and Principal-level B2B SaaS product roles, with a preference for enterprise software companies where your background is directly relevant. You prefer Bay Area or remote roles and have a reasonably clear view of the type of work and compensation you want.

During the last several months, however, you have gradually expanded beyond that target. You estimated that only about half of your recent applications closely match the target you described.

You are currently submitting approximately 35-45 applications per month. AI is helping you move faster, particularly with resume tailoring and cover letters.

You also described lower search energy than when you started and increasing urgency to find your next role. Those circumstances are not part of your behavioral score, but they help explain why your search has shifted toward greater application volume and broader targeting.

What stands out

Your search effort and your interview outcomes are not distributed the same way.

Cold applications consume most of your time but have generated relatively few conversations. Relationships and recruiters consume much less effort but account for most of the interviews you described.

That makes Channel Strategy one of the clearest areas to experiment with.

Behavioral Findings

Strength1. Target Clarity: 88 / 100

You can clearly describe the role, level, company environment, work characteristics, location, and compensation you want. Your target is specific enough to guide decisions.

Watch-out: A clear target only creates value when it influences which opportunities receive your time.

Opportunity2. Target Adherence: 54 / 100

Your stated target is considerably narrower than your actual application portfolio. You described broadening into lower-level roles and adjacent industries as the search continued. Some of this is deliberate experimentation, but some appears to be driven by urgency and the desire to maintain application volume.

Signal to watch: Track the percentage of applications that meet your own definition of a strong target rather than simply tracking total applications.

Moderate3. Opportunity Qualification: 61 / 100

You generally check role requirements and recognize obvious mismatches, but you do not consistently make an explicit go/no-go assessment before investing time. One revealing signal was difficulty recalling roles you deliberately decided not to pursue.

Opportunity: Before applying, force a short decision: Why is this opportunity worth 30-60 minutes of my search time?

Moderate4. Application Intentionality: 58 / 100

Your effort varies substantially by opportunity. For high-interest roles, you research, adjust positioning, and review the application carefully. For many cold applications, AI has enabled a much faster workflow. That efficiency is useful, but it also appears to have lowered the threshold for submitting applications.

Relative strength5. Story-to-Role Alignment: 72 / 100

When you deliberately position yourself, you can identify the two or three experiences that make you credible for the role and support them with concrete outcomes. Your successful application example showed this clearly.

Opportunity: Make this behavior the default for high-priority opportunities rather than something reserved for unusually attractive roles.

Largest opportunity6. Channel Strategy: 46 / 100

Your effort is concentrated in the channel producing the weakest observed results. You already have evidence that recruiters, referrals, and existing relationships generate substantially more conversations for you than cold applications.

The issue is therefore less about discovering a new tactic and more about reallocating effort toward a tactic already working for you.

Opportunity7. Relationship Activation: 52 / 100

You have a potentially valuable professional network, but relationship activation is episodic rather than systematic. Your strongest examples involved specific, personalized conversations rather than generic requests for referrals. There appears to be room to create a repeatable relationship workflow.

Moderate8. Human-Directed AI Use: 64 / 100

You use AI productively for research, rewriting, and tailoring. You generally retain control of the underlying facts and positioning. However, as application volume increased, you described reviewing some AI-tailored materials less carefully.

The risk is not AI itself. The risk is allowing AI's ability to produce an application quickly to change the decision threshold for whether an application deserves to be submitted at all.

Moderate9. Learning and Adaptation: 57 / 100

You recognize that referrals and recruiters have been more productive, but your allocation of time has not yet changed proportionally. You are learning from the search, but the feedback loop between results and behavior could be tighter.

That loop may feel productive because application volume increases, while simultaneously reducing the amount of effort going toward the opportunities most likely to generate conversations.

Three Experiments for the Next 30 Days

Experiment 1: Shift from application volume to qualified opportunities

Before applying, classify each opportunity:

  • A - Strong fit: clearly matches target and you can immediately explain why you should be interviewed.
  • B - Reasonable fit: meaningful overlap but one or two important gaps.
  • C - Stretch: significant mismatch or outside the target.

For the next 30 days, aim for most application effort to go toward A opportunities. Do not optimize for applications submitted. Track qualified opportunities pursued.

Experiment 2: Put human contact in front of high-value applications

For every A opportunity, spend 10-15 minutes looking for a credible human path before submitting:

  • Former colleague
  • First- or second-degree connection
  • Recruiter
  • Hiring manager
  • Employee in the relevant organization
  • Professional community connection

The objective is not necessarily to obtain a formal referral. The objective is to increase the number of strong-fit opportunities where someone knows who you are before or shortly after your application arrives.

Experiment 3: Require a human review checkpoint after AI

Continue using AI, but introduce one rule:

AI can prepare the application. You make the submission decision.

Before submitting an A opportunity, answer three questions yourself:

  1. Why am I a strong candidate for this specific role?
  2. Are those reasons obvious in the first half of my resume?
  3. Did I personally verify and approve everything AI changed?

If you cannot answer the first question clearly, reconsider whether the opportunity deserves an application.

What to Measure

For the next 30 days, track a small funnel rather than just application count:

Qualified Opportunities → Applications / Outreach → Human Responses → Recruiter Conversations → Interviews

Also record the source of each conversation. A simple weekly dashboard could look like this:

The most useful metric is likely to become: Interviews generated per 10 qualified opportunities pursued. That lets you improve the effectiveness of the search rather than simply increasing its volume.

Your Next Benchmark

Your current report establishes a baseline. After another 30 days, the useful questions are not simply whether you applied to more jobs. They are:

  • Did a larger percentage of your effort go toward strong-fit opportunities?
  • Did more high-value opportunities include a human connection?
  • Did your interview-generation rate improve?
  • Which channels produced the highest return?
  • Did AI save time without reducing judgment or application quality?

As the study grows, these behavioral scores can also be compared with participants pursuing similar roles and, more importantly, with similar participants who are generating interviews at a higher rate.

Bottom Line

Your search does not appear to suffer primarily from lack of activity. The stronger hypothesis from this interview is effort allocation.

You already demonstrated a pattern that produces conversations: stronger-fit opportunities + deliberate positioning + human connection + careful execution. Your next experiment should be to make that pattern a larger percentage of your search rather than increasing overall application volume.
