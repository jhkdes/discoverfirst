challenges we solve

  • 01

    Sales and CSMs block customer access

    Account teams control customer relationships and won't let you talk directly — protecting deals, avoiding surprises, or simply not seeing research as their problem.

  • 02

    What customers say doesn't match what they do

    Enterprise buyers are polite and deferential. Interview data and in-app behavioral data from the same users often tell completely different stories.

  • 03

    Leadership skips discovery and dictates the roadmap

    Even when you have the skills, you don't always have permission. Top-down roadmaps, fixed timelines, and command-and-control cultures treat discovery as a luxury.

  • 04

    Insights get shelved before they influence anything

    Running interviews is only half the problem. Getting findings to influence what gets built — past leadership skepticism and existing commitments — is the other half.

  • 05

    Too many stakeholders, too few clear problem owners

    Enterprise buying groups now average 11+ stakeholders. The person who feels the pain daily is rarely the same as the buyer, the champion, or the decision-maker.

  • 06

    Prototype quality wars with sales

    PMs need rough, throwaway prototypes. Sales insists on polished demos. The result: over-engineered prototypes that generate feedback on execution, not the underlying problem.

  • 07

    Invisible problems and slow feedback cycles

    Enterprise users normalize workarounds and rarely articulate root causes. With 6–18 month sales cycles, feedback loops are slow and validation arrives long after you needed it.

  • 08

    Early commitments limit future discovery

    Architectural, contractual, and roadmap commitments made to early customers quietly reduce your ability to pivot. Discovery gets harder as the product matures.

"The most common blocker I heard across all my client work was: my sales team doesn't want me to talk to a customer."

— Teresa Torres, author of Continuous Discovery Habits
