How the interview starts

“Thinking about the past two weeks, tell me about the last time you thought about taking a step toward a new role. What was going on, and what happened next?”

Then we follow your story. There are no right answers, and an example can be small: exploring a role, talking with someone, trying something, or deciding to wait.

Who we're looking for

We want to hear from everyone in the job market.

We're focusing on two groups. Whether you're still deciding what to do next or already applying, and whether or not you have a job right now, your experience counts too.

Our current focus

Recent college graduates

People with limited full-time professional experience who are moving toward a first or next role.

Experienced professionals

People with 15 or more years of experience who are moving toward a new role.

What we want to understand

What gets in the way, and what people do about it.

The study is for people building tools and services for job seekers, who need to know which problems deserve a closer look. We ask about three areas.

01

Sustaining the search

What affects someone's ability to start, keep going, or recover after a setback?

02

Choosing a direction

How do people figure out what they want, and test whether a role or work environment fits?

03

Getting traction

Where do people get stuck finding roles, reaching people, showing what they can do, applying, or interviewing?

How it works

A short questionnaire, then your story.

1

Answer a few questions

Mostly multiple choice: your experience, your work situation, how long you've been searching, and what you've done in the past month.

2

Tell us about a recent step

An AI voice interviewer asks about the last time you thought about moving toward a new role, and follows your story from there. Plan on 15 to 20 minutes.

3

Walk us through the friction

Where something got hard, we ask for specifics: what you sent, said or tried, what response you got, and what you did next.

4

Get the roundup, and connect if you like

Everyone who takes part gets the tools and tactics roundup once the study is complete. After the interview, you can also opt in to meet another job seeker.

What you get in return

A roundup for everyone, and a connection if you want one.

Everyone who takes part gets the roundup. Meeting another job seeker is optional.

For everyone who takes part

Peer tools and tactics

A short roundup of the tools, resources and tactics other job seekers actually used, with the context and what each one helped them do. We share it once the study is complete.

Optional

A conversation with another job seeker

If you'd like it, we'll introduce you to a job seeker at a similar level, for networking, when a suitable match is available. You both opt in before anyone's contact details are shared.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What do I get for taking part?

Everyone who takes part gets a roundup of the tools and tactics shared by other job seekers, once the study is complete. If you'd like, you can also be connected with job seekers at a similar level for networking. That part is optional: it only happens if you and the other person both opt in, and we share contact details only then.

I'm not a recent graduate or an experienced professional. Can I take part?

Yes. We're focusing on two groups, but we want to hear from everyone in the job market.

What if I haven't started searching yet?

That's fine. We want to hear from people who are still deciding what to do next, as well as people who are already applying.

What if I'm still employed?

We're talking with people who are searching while employed and people who are not working. Your work situation is one of the questions at the start.

How long does it take, and what do I need?

About 15 to 20 minutes. It's a voice conversation with an AI interviewer, so use a device with a microphone and find a quiet spot.

Do I need to prepare?

No. We ask about a recent, real example. If nothing comes to mind, we'll ask for the most recent one you remember.

Do I have to talk about money, confidence or how I feel?

No. If those come up, we ask how they affected a specific decision or action, and you choose how much to share.

Is the interview recorded, and who sees what I say?

Yes, it's recorded. Recordings are not shared publicly, and all answers and quotes are anonymized.

What happens with what I say?

We look for patterns across interviews and report how many participants described each one, so readers can judge the evidence. All answers and quotes are anonymized.

Who is running the study?

Discover First. Questions about the study can go to jkim@discoverfirst.co.
